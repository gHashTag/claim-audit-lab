## 2. Programme claims (verbatim)

**Klimesch (foundational observation, Pletzer-Kerschbaum-Klimesch 2010):**

> "The classical frequency bands of the EEG can be described as a geometric series with a ratio
> (between neighbouring frequencies) of 1.618, which is the golden mean."
> -- Pletzer, Kerschbaum & Klimesch (2010). Brain Research 1335:91-102.
> https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20350536/

> "A synchronization of the excitatory phases of two oscillations with frequencies f1 and f2 is
> impossible (in a mathematical sense) when their ratio equals the golden mean, because their
> excitatory phases never meet."
> -- Pletzer, Kerschbaum & Klimesch (2010). Same source.

**Klimesch (2013, extending to a body-frequency architecture):**

> "The width of a band is defined on the basis of the 'golden mean role' ... to guarantee minimal
> interference between bands."
> -- Klimesch (2013). Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 7:766.
> https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00766/full

> "The frequency of 1.25 Hz equals 75 beats per minute (bpm) which is very close to the average
> heart rate (HR) of young adults. This suggests that HR ... is the basic frequency and the scaling
> factor for all other frequency domains."
> -- Klimesch (2013). Same source.

**Kramer (2022, theoretical optimality conjecture):**

> "Here we propose that rhythms separated by factors of the golden ratio
> (phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2) optimally support segregation and cross-frequency integration of information
> transmission in the brain."
> -- Kramer (2022). Neurons, Behavior, Data Analysis and Theory.
> https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181851/

> "Golden rhythms -- rhythms organized by the golden ratio -- are the optimal choice to integrate
> information among separate rhythmic communication channels."
> -- Kramer (2022). Same source.

> "For a system of damped coupled oscillators with oscillator and gain frequencies spaced by the
> multiplicative factor c, cross-frequency coupling between three neighboring rhythms requires
> c = phi, the golden ratio."
> -- Kramer (2022). Same source (derivation result).

> "The fourth hypothesis ... remain[s] speculation, without robust supporting evidence."
> -- Kramer (2022). Same source (own qualification of the weakest hypothesis).

## 4. [Verified] inventory

- **[Verified]** The mathematical theorem in Pletzer, Kerschbaum & Klimesch (2010): two oscillators
  whose frequency ratio equals phi (the golden mean) cannot achieve phase synchronisation in the
  strict mathematical sense, because the irrationality of phi (specifically, phi being the "most
  irrational" number in the sense of slowest continued-fraction convergence) implies their
  excitatory phases never coincide exactly. Source:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20350536/ . Evidence: the result follows from the irrationality
  of phi and properties of continued-fraction approximation -- derivable from first principles of
  number theory. Independently well-established in the mathematical literature on irrational
  rotations. This is [Verified] as mathematics. It is NOT [Verified] that the brain uses this
  property.

- **[Verified]** The algebraic derivation in Kramer (2022) that for a geometric frequency
  progression {f_k = f_0 * c^k}, cross-frequency coupling between three neighboring rhythms
  (satisfying f_{k+2} = f_{k+1} + f_k) requires c^2 - c - 1 = 0, whose positive solution is
  c = phi. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181851/ . Evidence: direct algebraic
  identity -- substituting the geometric progression into the resonance condition yields the
  characteristic equation of phi. Re-derivable in a few lines. This is [Verified] as an algebraic
  result for the model. It is NOT [Verified] that real brain oscillators satisfy the model's
  coupling assumptions.

## 5. [Empirical fit] inventory

- **[Empirical fit]** Klimesch's observation that canonical EEG band centres (delta ~2.5 Hz, theta
  ~5 Hz, alpha ~10 Hz, beta ~20 Hz, gamma ~40 Hz) approximate a geometric series with ratio ~2
  (powers of 2 times the base), and that band widths are governed by a phi-based "golden mean
  rule." The specific claim that the ratio between *neighbouring frequencies* (not band centres
  themselves) equals 1.618 depends on (a) the choice of base frequency -- Klimesch proposes heart
  rate ~1.25 Hz as the anchor, (b) which EEG bands are included in the series, and (c) the
  tolerance on what counts as "approximately phi." Sources:
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20350536/ (Pletzer-Klimesch 2010);
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00766/full
  (Klimesch 2013). Free parameters: base frequency choice (k=1), band-inclusion criterion (k=1),
  tolerance threshold (k=1). Control: a matched-control comparison against other geometric series
  (e.g., ratio 2, ratio e) with equal parameter count has not been published as part of the
  programme. Pre-registered held-out test: absent from the original publications; the Ursachi 2026
  study constitutes the first systematic independent test.

- **[Empirical fit]** Ursachi (2026), an independent replication study (author affiliated as
  Independent Researcher, Bucharest, Romania -- no institutional connection to Kramer or Klimesch),
  applied the Phi Coupling Index (PCI) to N=320 subjects across two independent datasets
  (PhysioNet EEGBCI, N=109; LEMON Mind-Brain-Body, N=211). Results: 80.0% of subjects showed
  phi-organisation (PCI > 0) with mean alpha/theta ratio 1.677 (cf. phi = 1.618); PCI-convergence
  correlation r=0.54, p<10^-25; replicated in both datasets (r=0.628 and r=0.497 respectively);
  frontal theta validation r=0.718. Source:
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2026.1781338/full
  . Free parameters: choice to focus on theta-alpha pair (rather than all adjacent band pairs),
  construction of the PCI metric itself (which compares distance-to-phi against distance-to-2.0
  rather than against a richer set of candidate ratios), and the convergence metric definition.
  Control: the phi-specificity parameter sweep (testing reference constants from 1.3 to 2.2) showed
  that the correlation peaked near phi = 1.618, with a decline at other values -- this constitutes
  a partial matched-control test, though only two reference points (phi vs. 2:1 harmonic) are used
  in the main PCI construction. A full matched-control comparison against all candidate irrational
  ratios at equal parameter count remains absent. Pre-registered held-out test: absent (study is
  observational; both datasets were included in the primary analysis). Independence of the
  replication author from the programme is confirmed.

## 6. [Open conjecture] inventory

- **[Open conjecture]** Kramer's optimality conjecture: brain rhythms organised by phi are the
  *optimal* choice for supporting cross-frequency integration, and this is WHY the observed EEG
  band structure has the spacing it does. This goes beyond the [Verified] mathematics (which shows
  that phi is a solution to the resonance condition for three-way coupling in the model) to the
  claim that the brain has converged on phi because of this property. Source:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181851/ . **Fpath (derivable from Kramer 2022):**
  the hypothesis is falsified if EEG bands in healthy subjects cluster around non-phi ratios, or if
  an alternative frequency spacing (e.g., ratio sqrt(2) ~= 1.414 or ratio e^(1/2) ~= 1.649) is
  shown to produce superior cross-frequency coupling in matched neural circuit models. Kramer
  explicitly states that a fourth sub-hypothesis "remains speculation, without robust supporting
  evidence," demonstrating awareness of the [Open conjecture] status of the functional
  interpretation. Executable: EEG datasets (including PhysioNet and LEMON used in Ursachi 2026)
  are publicly available; matched-control simulations are computationally tractable on a standard
  workstation.

## 7. [Risk] / [High-risk] inventory

- **[Risk]** The matched-control comparison is absent from the core programme publications. The
  claim that phi is *specifically* the organising ratio -- rather than any nearby irrational number
  -- requires showing that a model using, say, ratio 1.55 or ratio 1.70 performs materially worse.
  The Ursachi 2026 phi-specificity sweep (testing reference constants 1.3 to 2.2) provides partial
  evidence of phi-specificity for the theta-alpha pair, but the sweep measures a derived index
  (PCI-convergence correlation) rather than a direct model comparison against alternative
  functionally motivated ratios. The current evidence base cannot rule out that the empirical
  theta-alpha ratio (~1.677, mean across N=320) is equally well described by a near-phi but
  non-phi irrational number with comparably motivated theoretical properties. Reason:
  FRAMEWORK.md [Risk] criterion (c) -- a plausible control (alternative irrational spacing) is
  not yet ruled out. Source:
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2026.1781338/full
  (discussion section).

- **[Risk]** The physiological bridge claim -- that heart rate (~1.25 Hz) is the anchor from
  which all EEG bands derive by integer-power-of-2 scaling with phi-governed bandwidths
  (Klimesch 2013) -- introduces an additional free parameter (the choice of heart rate as the
  biologically privileged base) that is not tested against alternative anchors (e.g., respiratory
  rate ~0.25 Hz, or an internal oscillator). This is an [Empirical fit] with an uncontrolled
  free parameter, not yet a falsified or confirmed physiological fact. Reason: FRAMEWORK.md
  [Risk] criterion (b) -- look-elsewhere correction missing for the anchor-frequency choice.
  Source:
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00766/full .

## 10. Audit summary

**Strongest part.** The [Verified] mathematical core -- the Pletzer-Kerschbaum-Klimesch 2010 proof
that phi-ratio frequencies cannot phase-lock (in the strict mathematical sense) and Kramer's
algebraic derivation that phi is the unique solution to the three-way resonance condition in a
geometric oscillator model -- is the clearest example in the entire catalog of [Verified]
mathematics supporting a phi-related theoretical proposal. The programme correctly keeps the
mathematical layer separate from the empirical claim.

**Weakest claim.** The physiological bridge claim (Klimesch 2013) that heart rate is the anchor
frequency from which all EEG bands derive by phi-governed scaling is [Risk] for look-elsewhere on
the anchor-frequency choice, and is not independently tested against alternative physiological
anchors.

**Single experiment that would advance the largest claim.** A pre-registered, matched-control EEG
analysis testing phi alongside at least two alternative geometric series ratios (e.g., 1.55, sqrt(2),
and 1.70) in a held-out dataset, measuring which ratio best predicts band-centre spacing across all
five canonical EEG bands in a sample with N >= 200 and pre-specified tolerance thresholds. This
experiment would either promote the core [Open conjecture] to [Empirical fit] with strong control,
or establish that phi is not specifically privileged over nearby alternatives.

**Epistemic status summary.** This is what a careful phi-empirical programme looks like at the
state of the art. The current epistemic status is [Empirical fit] for the observation (EEG band
centres approximately phi-spaced) and [Open conjecture] with executable Fpath for the optimality
conjecture (phi is WHY the brain chose this spacing). Promotion to [Verified] requires
matched-control replication in additional independent groups with pre-specified tolerance thresholds
and a comparison against alternative candidate ratios. The symmetric position of the lab's own work
is: our [Open conjecture] (breadth-as-moat) is in the same state -- motivated, falsifier specified,
decisive experiment pending.
